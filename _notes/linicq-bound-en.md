Tabular foundation models such as TabPFN and LimiX read a handful of labelled context rows and one unlabelled query row in a single forward pass. This note defines a purely numeric tabular task in order to ask a narrow question: **when the context already determines the answer, can a fixed-depth single pass still fail to compute it?**

The answer is a conditional yes. The construction has two steps: write linear solving as a tabular episode, then show that this task contains an NC1-hard subfamily, while a fixed-depth single pass sits inside TC0.

## Problem setup

Consider a tabular episode made of labelled context samples and one query sample whose label is hidden. With feature dimension $m$, the context is

$$
\mathcal{D}_{C}=\{(a_i,b_i)\}_{i=1}^{m},\qquad a_i\in\mathbb{R}^{m},\ b_i\in\mathbb{R},
$$

and the query is

$$
\mathcal{D}_{Q}=\{(q,\mathsf{MASK})\},\qquad q\in\mathbb{R}^{m}.
$$

Here $\mathsf{MASK}$ marks the unknown query label. We promise that a single unknown parameter vector $\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{m}$ satisfies every context row exactly:

$$
a_i^{\top}\theta=b_i,\qquad i=1,\dots,m,
$$

and the task is to predict the query label $y_Q=q^{\top}\theta$ from the context.

## Linear In-Context Query

**Definition (LinICQ).** Let

$$
A\in\{-1,0,1\}^{m\times m},\qquad b\in\{0,1\}^{m},\qquad q\in\{0,1\}^{m},
$$

with $A$ invertible, and set $\theta=A^{-1}b$. The context consists of the rows of $A$ with labels $b$, the query is $q$, and the model must output

$$
y_Q=q^{\top}A^{-1}b .
$$

In the hard subfamily used for the binary lower bound we further promise $q^{\top}A^{-1}b\in\{0,1\}$.

Joining context and query, the whole episode is an ordinary numeric table:

$$
T(A,b,q)=
\begin{bmatrix}
A & b\\
q^{\top} & \mathsf{MASK}
\end{bmatrix}.
$$

The first $m$ rows are the context, the last row is the query; the first $m$ columns are features and the last column is the label column.

### Matching the tabular interfaces

For models with a train/test interface one can take $X_{\text{train}}=A$, $y_{\text{train}}=b$, $X_{\text{test}}=q^{\top}$.

For models with a joint masked-table interface one feeds $T(A,b,q)$ directly. The query label slot is encoded as NaN, a missing value, or a dedicated MASK embedding, but the true $y_Q$ never enters the input.

### Why this is a tabular-native task

Every context row $(a_i,b_i)$ is a standard numeric feature–label sample. The equation $a_i^{\top}\theta=b_i$ is only the algebraic regularity these supervised samples share, not an "equation string" in natural language.

Context rows can be permuted freely; if one simultaneously permutes the columns of $A$, the coordinates of $q$ and the coordinates of $\theta$, the answer is unchanged. The task therefore does not depend on the absolute order of natural-language tokens.

## A hard subfamily generated by a finite automaton

To show that LinICQ contains long sequential computation, fix a deterministic finite automaton

$$
D=(S,\Sigma,\delta,s_0,F),
$$

where $S$ is the state set, $\Sigma$ the alphabet, $\delta:S\times\Sigma\to S$ the transition function, $s_0$ the initial state and $F\subseteq S$ the accepting states. The input string is $w=\omega_1\omega_2\cdots\omega_n$.

Choose a fixed automaton whose membership problem is NC1-complete. We encode "does $D$ accept $w$" as a LinICQ episode.

### State variables

For every time $i\in\{0,1,\dots,n\}$ and state $s\in S$, define

$$
z_{i,s}=\mathbf{1}\{\text{the automaton is in state } s \text{ after reading } \omega_1\cdots\omega_i\}.
$$

Since the automaton is in exactly one state at each time, the vector $z_i=(z_{i,s})_{s\in S}$ is one-hot and satisfies

$$
\sum_{s\in S} z_{i,s}=1 .
$$

### Initial condition and transition equations

The initial state is fixed by

$$
z_{0,s_0}=1,\qquad z_{0,s}=0\quad(s\neq s_0).
$$

After reading the $i$-th symbol $\omega_i$, for each target state $s\in S$ write

$$
z_{i,s}-\sum_{r:\,\delta(r,\omega_i)=s} z_{i-1,r}=0 .
$$

The sum runs over all predecessors $r$ that move to $s$ on $\omega_i$. Because $z_{i-1}$ is one-hot, the sum is either $0$ or $1$, and it records exactly whether step $i$ reaches state $s$.

### The accepting variable

Add an unknown variable $u$ and write

$$
u-\sum_{s\in F} z_{n,s}=0 .
$$

Since the final state vector $z_n$ is one-hot,

$$
u=\mathbf{1}\{w\in L(D)\}.
$$

The variable $u$ is an unknown output determined by the system; it is not written into the context as a known answer.

### Matrix form

Order the unknowns by time:

$$
\theta_w=(z_0,z_1,\dots,z_n,u)^{\top}.
$$

All equations combine into $A_w\theta_w=b_w$, where $A_w$ is block lower triangular with identity diagonal blocks:

$$
A_w=
\begin{bmatrix}
I & 0 & 0 & \cdots & 0\\
-M_{\omega_1} & I & 0 & \cdots & 0\\
0 & -M_{\omega_2} & I & \cdots & 0\\
\vdots & & \ddots & \ddots & \vdots\\
0 & \cdots & 0 & -c_F^{\top} & 1
\end{bmatrix}.
$$

Here $M_{\omega_i}$ is the state-transition matrix of symbol $\omega_i$ and $c_F$ is the indicator vector of accepting states. Every diagonal block is the identity, so

$$
\det(A_w)=1,
$$

and $A_w$ is invertible with a unique solution.

Let $e_u$ be the basis vector selecting $u$ and set the query to $q=e_u$. Then

$$
q^{\top}A_w^{-1}b_w=u=\mathbf{1}\{w\in L(D)\}.
$$

An automaton membership instance $w$ has thus been turned into a purely numeric context/query table prediction instance.

## A conditional one-shot bound

Let $N$ be the binary encoding length of the whole tabular episode. Consider a family of TabPFN/LimiX-style axial tabular transformers under the following resource limits:

$$
L=O(1),\qquad p(N)=O(\log N),\qquad \operatorname{size}(M_N)=\operatorname{poly}(N),
$$

where $L$ is the block depth, $p(N)$ the internal numerical precision, and the model performs a single non-recursive forward pass.

If embedding, cross-feature attention, cross-sample attention, FFN, residual, normalization and output comparison all satisfy the corresponding finite-precision circuit simulation conditions, then

$$
\text{Embed},\ \text{FeatureAttn},\ \text{SampleAttn},\ \text{FFN/Norm/Residual}\in \mathrm{TC}^0 .
$$

Composing a fixed number of components and a fixed number of blocks stays in TC0, hence

$$
\mathcal{F}_{\text{TabularTransformer}}\subseteq \mathrm{TC}^0 .
$$

On the other hand, the map

$$
\rho:\ w\mapsto (A_w,b_w,e_u)
$$

is computable in uniform AC0 (and, under a suitable encoding, by a weaker local circuit), because each coefficient of $A_w$ depends only on the fixed transition table, on fixed row and column positions, and on at most one input symbol $\omega_i$. The reduction never precomputes the automaton's run.

> **Theorem.** Assume $\mathrm{TC}^0\neq\mathrm{NC}^1$. Then no scalable family of TabPFN/LimiX-style axial tabular transformers can simultaneously have fixed block depth, $O(\log N)$-bit precision, polynomial total size and a single non-recursive forward pass, and exactly predict the query label for every scale and every legal episode of the hard LinICQ subfamily above.

**Proof idea.** Suppose such a family $M$ exists. For any automaton membership instance $w$, first build the table $T_w=T(A_w,b_w,e_u)$ with $\rho\in\mathrm{AC}^0$, then call $M$ to obtain

$$
M(T_w)=e_u^{\top}A_w^{-1}b_w=\mathbf{1}\{w\in L(D)\}.
$$

Since $\mathrm{AC}^0\subseteq\mathrm{TC}^0$ and TC0 is closed under constant composition, the NC1-complete automaton membership problem would lie in TC0, so $\mathrm{NC}^1\subseteq\mathrm{TC}^0$. Combined with the standard inclusion $\mathrm{TC}^0\subseteq\mathrm{NC}^1$ this gives $\mathrm{TC}^0=\mathrm{NC}^1$, contradicting the assumption.

### What the bound does and does not say

The conclusion is conditional, worst-case, asymptotic, exact and one-shot. It does not say that a particular finite checkpoint fails on all small instances, nor does it imply low average accuracy, the impossibility of approximation, or impossibility after adding several rounds of iteration.

### An explicit hard automaton

Different fixed DFAs can have different membership complexity. By Barrington's theorem and standard uniform corollaries, the fixed language

$$
WPe(S_5)=\{g_1\cdots g_n\in S_5^{*}: g_1g_2\cdots g_n=e\}
$$

is complete for DLOGTIME-uniform NC1 under DLOGTIME-uniform AC0 many-one reductions. It is recognised by the fixed DFA

$$
D_{\text{hard}}=(S_5,S_5,\delta,e,\{e\}),\qquad \delta(q,g)=qg .
$$

Using one explicit hard DFA as the source of the reduction does not claim that all DFAs have the same complexity.

For fixed state numbering, symbol numbering and table layout, every output cell of $\rho_n:w\mapsto T(A_w,b_w,e_u)$ depends only on fixed coordinates, the fixed transition table and at most one input symbol. Hence $\rho_n$ is a DLOGTIME-uniform AC0 transducer: it writes coefficients and right-hand sides only, and never precomputes the state trajectory or the accepting answer.

## Closing

This bound only constrains a single non-recursive forward pass. Unfolding the state propagation into explicit reasoning steps, or merging transitions into a balanced tree, belongs to a different computational model whose upper bounds have to be discussed separately — that is the next step for this task.
